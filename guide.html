1. What Form WH-347 is (and who has to file it)

Form WH-347 is the U.S. Department of Labor's certified payroll report. If you're a contractor or subcontractor on a federally funded construction project — or a state job covered by a "little Davis-Bacon" law — you must submit certified payroll every week, for every week you perform work, until the project is done.

The report proves that each worker on the job was paid at least the prevailing wage and fringe benefits set by the applicable wage determination. Get it wrong or file it late, and your payment can be withheld and penalties can follow. That's why this weekly form matters far more than its size suggests.

The short version: one row per worker, hours broken out by day, straight-time and overtime separated, gross pay, deductions, and net pay — then a signed statement certifying it's all true.

2. What you need before you start

  • Your wage determination for the project (from SAM.gov) — this lists the required base rate and fringe for each labor classification.
  • Each worker's name, an individual identifying number (last 4 of SSN is now standard — do not put the full SSN on the form), and work classification.
  • Daily hours worked on this project for the week.
  • The hourly rate paid and how fringe benefits are handled (paid into plans, or paid in cash).
  • All deductions (FICA, federal tax, state tax, and any others).

4. The payroll columns, explained

Each worker is one block of rows. Here is what every column means:

ColumnWhat to enter
(1) Name and Individual Identifying NumberWorker's name and the last 4 digits of their SSN. Full address only needs to be on the first payroll a worker appears on.
(2) Withholding ExemptionsOptional. Most contractors leave this blank now.
(3) Work ClassificationThe exact classification from your wage determination (e.g., "Electrician," "Laborer Group 1"). This must match, or the report is wrong.
(4) Hours Worked Each DayDaily hours on this project, split into overtime (O) and straight time (S) rows.
(5) Total HoursThe week's total for straight time and for overtime.
(6) Rate of PayThe base hourly rate. If fringe is paid in cash, the cash fringe is shown here too.
(7) Gross Amount EarnedTotal earned. If the worker worked on other projects that week, show "this project / all projects."
(8) DeductionsFICA, federal tax, state tax, other — and the total.
(9) Net Wages PaidGross minus deductions. What the worker actually received.

5. Overtime and the fringe-benefit trap

This is where most WH-347s go wrong. Two rules to hold onto:

Overtime is 1.5× the base rate — not the base + fringe

When a worker goes over 40 hours, overtime is 1.5 times the basic cash rate only. The fringe-benefit amount is added at its straight value on top — it is not multiplied by 1.5. Multiplying the whole loaded rate by 1.5 overpays and flags the report as incorrect.

Fringe benefits: plans vs. cash

On the Statement of Compliance you must declare whether fringe was paid into approved plans, funds, or programs (4a) or paid in cash (4b). If some was cash and some was plans, you note the split under exceptions. This election is not optional — pick the one that matches how you actually pay.

Reminder: the required base rate and fringe come from your project's wage determination on SAM.gov. This guide explains the form; it can't tell you the correct rate for your classification and locality — always verify against your determination.

6. The Statement of Compliance (page 2)

Page 2 is a signed certification. A company officer fills in the week-ending date, the company name, and their name and title, then signs. By signing, they certify that:

  • The payroll for that week is correct and complete.
  • Each worker was paid at least the required prevailing wage for their classification.
  • Fringe benefits were paid as declared (into plans, or in cash).
  • No unauthorized deductions were taken.

Knowingly signing a false Statement of Compliance carries civil and criminal penalties, which is exactly why accuracy on page 1 matters.

7. The 6 most common mistakes

  1. Multiplying fringe by 1.5 for overtime. Only the base rate gets 1.5×.
  2. Classification doesn't match the wage determination. "Helper" is not a valid classification unless it's on the determination.
  3. Skipping a week. You file for every week work is performed — even a partial week — and mark the final one.
  4. Full SSN on the form. Use only the last 4 digits; keep full identifying info in your own records.
  5. Forgetting the Statement of Compliance. Page 1 without the signed page 2 is incomplete.
  6. Not showing "this project vs. all projects" gross when a worker split time across jobs that week.

8. Frequently asked questions

What is Form WH-347?

It's the Department of Labor's standard certified payroll report, filed weekly on Davis-Bacon covered construction jobs to document prevailing-wage compliance.

Is the WH-347 form mandatory?

Submitting certified payroll weekly is mandatory. The WH-347 format itself is optional, but it's the standard that satisfies the requirement, so nearly everyone uses it.

How do I calculate overtime on certified payroll?

1.5× the basic hourly rate for hours over 40, with the fringe amount added at its straight value on top — not multiplied.

Can I fill out WH-347 in Excel?

You can, but the daily hour splits, overtime math, and per-worker totals are where errors creep in. A tool that does the arithmetic and prints the exact form removes that risk.
